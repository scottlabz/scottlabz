A campaign runs for a full quarter, spend goes out every week, and when someone finally asks what it actually produced, the honest answer is a shrug and a screenshot of a dashboard nobody fully trusts.

It's rarely a bad campaign. It's a campaign launched without a clear tracking setup underneath it, so results get read off a platform's own reporting instead of what actually happened on the site - a lead form that didn't get credited, a sale attributed to the wrong channel, a number that looks fine but doesn't tie back to anything real.

Campaign work starts with the tracking, not the ad. Conversions get connected back to the campaign, the audience, and the creative that caused them, so a budget decision is based on what actually drove a result, not on which report is easiest to read.

From there, budget moves toward what's proven to work. Underperforming campaigns get caught early instead of running out a quarter on autopilot, and the channels producing real results get more room, on a review schedule that matches how the business actually makes decisions.

This isn't a hand-off to a set-it-and-forget vendor. Campaigns get managed day-to-day - creative, targeting, and bids adjusted as real performance data comes in, not reviewed once a month after the budget's already spent.

For businesses in Bloomington-Normal and across McLean County, this work is available locally, with the option to meet in person and walk through what's currently running and what it's actually producing. Most engagements start by reviewing existing campaigns and tracking, since a program that's mostly working usually needs tighter measurement, not a restart.
