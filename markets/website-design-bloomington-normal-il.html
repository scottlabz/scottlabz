Most businesses in Bloomington-Normal already have a website - the question is whether it still represents them well. A site gets built once, usually years ago, and from there it mostly stays frozen while everything else about the business moves forward. New services get added as an afterthought, photos get swapped out but the layout never changes, and on a phone - where most visitors actually show up - the whole thing feels cramped and dated.

This isn't unusual. Websites are typically designed once, around a launch date, and then left alone once the immediate pressure is gone. Design trends move, customer expectations shift, and browsing habits keep tilting further toward mobile - but the site underneath rarely gets revisited to match. Over time, small gaps add up: a homepage that buries the one thing visitors actually came for, a contact form that's a chore to fill out on a phone, pages that take too long to load and lose people before they ever see the content.

A properly designed site closes that gap. Instead of stretching a years-old layout to cover new services or products, the site gets structured around how visitors actually browse - a clear path to the information they came for, a design that holds up on any screen size, and pages that load quickly instead of testing someone's patience. The goal isn't just a site that looks fresh on launch day. It's a site that still makes sense a few years from now, as the business keeps changing.

That work starts with a look at the current site: what's confusing, what's slow, and what's quietly turning visitors away before they ever reach out. From there, a design plan focuses on the handful of pages and paths that actually matter, rather than a redesign of everything at once. Every business is browsed differently - a service business might live or die by how easy it is to find a phone number, while a retailer needs product pages that are quick to scan and easy to shop. The design reflects that, instead of applying the same layout to every site.

Ongoing support keeps it that way. Instead of a site that starts drifting the moment it launches, small updates - new content, a seasonal promotion, a page that needs rethinking - get handled as they come up, so the site keeps matching the business instead of falling behind it. Regular attention also covers the changes that come from new devices, browser updates, and search engines that keep raising the bar on what a "modern" site looks like.

Projects are scoped around what a business actually needs visitors to see and do, on a timeline that fits how the business operates - a full new site, a redesign of an existing one, or targeted fixes to what's already live. Rather than a long list of features nobody asked for, the focus stays on the handful of things that make a real difference to how visitors experience the site.

For businesses in Bloomington-Normal and across McLean County, this kind of work is available locally, with the option to meet in person to walk through the current site and talk through what's working and what isn't. Most engagements start by reviewing what's already in place rather than starting over, since a working site usually needs targeted improvements more than a full rebuild.
