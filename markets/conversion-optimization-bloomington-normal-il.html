Conversion Optimization in Bloomington-Normal

A redesigned checkout page ships because it looks cleaner, and three weeks later nobody can say whether it helped, hurt, or did nothing. There was no baseline before the change and no test around it, just a launch and a shrug when someone asks how it's doing.

It's rarely one bad call. A button gets moved, a form gets shortened, a page gets redesigned - each change reasonable on its own, made on instinct because instinct is faster than a test. Enough of those stack up and nobody can trace which change actually moved a number, or whether any of them did.

Structured testing starts by watching how visitors actually move through a site - where they stall, where they leave, what they skip - before deciding what's worth changing. A hypothesis gets written down, a test gets run with real statistical guardrails, and a change earns its place by proving it moved the number it was supposed to move.

That process gets built around what's already live, not a rebuild. A checkout, a signup form, a product page - whatever's costing conversions gets tested in place, with a large enough sample and a long enough run to trust the result instead of calling it early because the numbers looked good on day two.

Regular review keeps it from becoming a one-time project. Traffic changes, seasons change, and a redesign elsewhere on the site can quietly undercut a test that was working. Checking in on a schedule, not just at launch, keeps optimization something that keeps paying off instead of fading after the first win.

For businesses in Bloomington-Normal and across McLean County, this work is available locally, with the option to meet in person and walk through current traffic and where it's actually stalling. Most engagements start by reviewing what's already live, since a site that's mostly working usually needs targeted testing, not a redesign.
