What Businesses Get Wrong About Their Website Data Back to Field Notes
Same gaps, different storefronts. A look at what's usually missing from business measurement setups, and what it quietly costs.

Why this exists

A lot of businesses are running on whatever measurement setup came installed by default - and nobody has ever gone back to check if it's actually working.

This isn't a knock on any one business. It's a pattern seen across a lot of sites, from small storefronts to established retailers, and it's worth naming plainly so it's easier to spot.

What the pattern looks like

Walk through enough business sites and the same handful of gaps show up again and again, regardless of how nice the site itself looks.

Traffic is tracked. Outcomes aren't.

Most sites have some kind of visitor counter running. Far fewer track whether a visit turned into a call, a form submission, a directions request, or a sale. Knowing that people showed up isn't the same as knowing what they came to do.

Every campaign looks the same in reporting

A business running a paid promotion, a sponsorship, and organic search traffic often can't tell which one actually brought someone in. Without basic source tagging, every visitor blends into one undifferentiated number.

Mobile behavior gets ignored

A large share of search traffic comes from a phone, usually someone looking for hours, a phone number, or directions. Sites rarely track whether that mobile visitor actually found what they needed before leaving.

Nobody's looked at it in years

Measurement tools get installed once during a site launch and then never revisited. Redesigns happen, pages get renamed, buttons move - and the tracking underneath quietly stops matching what's actually on the page.

Why this happens so often

It isn't a lack of effort. It's a lack of access. Many businesses end up choosing between a generalist web shop that treats measurement as an afterthought, or handling it themselves alongside everything else running a business requires.

Neither path is wrong. But it does mean the gap tends to persist far longer than it should, quietly, in the background, while everything else about the business moves forward.

What closing the gap actually looks like

None of this requires a rebuild. In most cases, it's a matter of:

  • Defining what actually counts as a meaningful outcome on the site - a call, a form, a booking, a purchase.
  • Tagging where visitors are coming from so campaigns can be told apart.
  • Checking that tracking still matches the site as it exists today, not as it existed at launch.
  • Setting up a simple, honest report that shows what's working without requiring a dashboard degree to read.

The goal isn't more data. It's knowing, with some confidence, what's actually bringing people in the door.

Frequently asked questions

Does this only apply to bigger businesses?

No. If anything, it matters more for smaller operations, where marketing budget is tighter and every dollar spent needs to be accounted for.

Do I need to replace what I'm already using?

Usually not. Most of this is about configuring what's already in place correctly, not swapping it out for something new.

How would I even know if this applies to my site?

A quick way to check: if a question like "which of our marketing efforts actually brought in customers last month" doesn't have a confident answer, that's usually a sign the underlying measurement isn't set up to answer it yet.

Not sure what your own site is telling you?

A short conversation is usually enough to tell whether there's a quick fix or a bigger gap worth addressing.
