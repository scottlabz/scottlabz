The basics

What accreditation means

Accreditation is granted by the Better Business Bureau to businesses that agree to uphold the BBB Standards for Trust - practices covering transparency, truthful advertising, and responsive handling of customer concerns. Accreditation is voluntary and reviewed on an ongoing basis rather than granted once and forgotten.

A BBB rating reflects factors such as time in operation, complaint history, and transparency of business practices. It is one signal among several, not a guarantee, and is intended to help a business be evaluated on its own track record - see our broader Security & Trust commitments for the rest of that picture.

Frequently asked questions

Is accreditation the same as a review score?

No. Accreditation reflects agreement to the BBB Standards for Trust. The letter rating is calculated separately, based on factors such as time in business, complaint history, and transparency.

Where can complaints or reviews be filed?

Both are handled directly through the BBB profile linked above, which keeps that record independent and unedited.
