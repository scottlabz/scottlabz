Kitchens Case Study

One Page That Sends Shoppers to Two Stores

Scott Labz rebuilt the website for Kitchens, a family-owned cookware store with locations in Iowa and Missouri, as a single page built to bring shoppers through the door, tell the story behind the shop and grow its mailing list.

Project Overview

Project A one-page website for a specialty kitchenware and gourmet food retailer
Organization Kitchens, a family-owned store selling cookware, cutlery, gourmet foods and fresh coffee since 1992
Locations Northpark Mall in Davenport, Iowa, and Branson Landing in Branson, Missouri
Client since 2016
Timeline Built September to October 2026. Live October 22, 2026
Services Design, development, store listings and search data, forms, analytics, accessibility, hosting, and the move from the old site
Live site mykitchensshop.com

The Challenge

Kitchens needed one page to do three jobs: send shoppers to its two stores, tell the story of a family-run shop that has served customers since 1992, and collect emails for its newsletter and promotions. It had to work as well on a phone as on a desktop, load quickly, and be simple for store staff to keep current.

The old site made each of those harder:

  • Store details were spread across separate pages for locations, brands, videos, recipes, the gallery, reviews, hiring and contact
  • Two stores in two states, each with its own hours, phone number, manager and coffee list
  • Old pages that customers' bookmarks and search results still pointed to
  • Both stores' email ran on the same domain, so the switch couldn't interrupt it

A new site answered each one, built from the design and content the client supplied, with nothing copied from the old site.

What Was Built

Scott Labz designed and built the site end to end: 23 rounds of design, the build, store listings for search, both forms, analytics, accessibility checks, and the switch from the old site.

A page built around the store visit

Found by search, shared cleanly

  • Each store is described for search engines with its address, phone, map location, photo and opening hours, tied to the business as a whole
  • The star ratings shown on the page are left out of that data, since search engines don't allow a business to mark up ratings taken from another site
  • A title and description that name both store cities, short enough to show in full in search results
  • Share tags and a share image, so a link to the site previews with its photo
  • A sitemap and robots file, and the domain verified for search with its sitemap submitted

Analytics that count visits, calls and sign-ups

  • One page view per visit: tapping a tab doesn't count as another page
  • Phone taps are counted by store and by where on the page they happened
  • Sign-ups and messages are counted only when they send, and a scroll near the bottom is counted too
  • The 404 page records the address that was missed, to find broken links

The switch from the old site

The new site replaced the old one on the same domain, with the store's email running on that domain throughout.

  • Every page from the old site, and anything under it, redirects to its matching section, so existing links and search rankings carry over
  • The www address and plain http both redirect to the one secure address, and anything else missing gets a 404 page with links home and to the stores
  • Only the four records that pointed at the old server changed. The other 25, including every email record, were compared before and after the switch and matched exactly
  • A full backup of the domain's records was taken just before the switch, with a one-command rollback ready if it had been needed
  • Files that change with each build are cached by browsers for a year, while the page itself is always re-checked, so updates show at once
  • Each change to the site goes live on its own in one to two minutes

The Results

Kitchens went live on October 22, 2026, with its forms, analytics and redirects in place from the first visit.

  • An automated accessibility scan found 0 violations on the home page and the 404 page
  • A page-quality audit of the live site scored 100 for accessibility, best practices and SEO, on both phone and desktop
  • The store listings in the search data passed a structured data validator with 0 errors and 0 warnings
  • The old site's pages redirect to their matching sections, and every email record matched before and after the switch
  • Every outside link was checked and matched to the right store, and all three videos are available

Business Impact

A store that has served customers since 1992 now has a site that does three jobs on one page: it gets shoppers to the right store, tells the story behind the shop, and grows the mailing list. The old site's links and search rankings carried over, and the store's email records were left exactly as they were.

Changing a price, an hour or a coffee flavor is now an edit to one data file. Through website development and measurement, Kitchens can see which store each phone call goes to, and how many sign-ups and messages the page brings in.
