Hawkeye Fan Shop Case Study

A Storefront Built to Handle Gameday, Not Just Survive It

A look at the measurement, product-page tracking, and site reliability work behind Hawkeye Fan Shop - the official online retailer for University of Iowa Athletics.

Project Overview

Project Ongoing eCommerce & Storefront Partnership
Organization Hawkeye Fan Shop, the official online retailer for University of Iowa Athletics
Focus Measurement Strategy, Product-Page Tracking, Site Reliability
Outcome Ten years of accurate reporting and a storefront that holds up on gameday

The Challenge

Hawkeye Fan Shop runs on a calendar it doesn't control. Traffic follows the Iowa Hawkeyes schedule rather than a steady curve, and every part of the storefront - from measurement to page speed - has to hold up when a single Saturday can outweigh a normal week.

Key challenges included:

  • Gameday traffic spikes that dwarf typical retail patterns
  • Reporting that needed to hold up across years, not just a single campaign
  • Product and catalog tracking detailed enough to inform real merchandising decisions across a catalog of thousands of products.
  • A decade-long relationship that meant carrying measurement forward without losing historical continuity

What Was Built

Rather than a single project, the work has been a continuous relationship - measurement rebuilt as tools changed, product tracking extended as the catalog grew, and the storefront kept steady through a decade of gamedays.

Category Navigation & Merchandising

Rather than one flat product catalog, the storefront is organized into a deep category structure - a dozen entry points like Jersey - Personal & NIL, Football, Tailgating & Gameday, and Collectibles, each acting as its own tracked path into the catalog rather than a generic browse link.

Twelve category tiles break the catalog into distinct entry points, from personalized jerseys to tailgating gear, with each tile's click-through tracked separately from the rest. The grid isn't static, either - tiles can be swapped, reordered, or re-themed on the fly to match the schedule, spotlighting a rivalry game or a homecoming week and then rotating back, all without touching the tracking already built around each category.

On mobile, this same grid trades a mile-long vertical scroll for a horizontally swipeable carousel - a smoother way to browse a dozen entry points on a small screen. Even in a quick swipe, tiles like Jersey - Personal & NIL (a customizable jersey builder) and Football (refreshed each year with new arrivals like this schedule magnet) carry enough detail to make stopping worth it.

Gift Cards & Store Pickup

The gift card and store network both get closer looks of their own. Each ties the online storefront back to something a fan can hold - a denomination-based gift card with real reviews attached, or a specific store with its own hours and contact info.

Checkout & Payment Options

At checkout, Hawkeye Fan Shop offers four express payment options beyond a standard card form - Shop Pay, PayPal, Google Pay, and Venmo - each shaving steps off the path from cart to completed order.

hawkeyefanshop.com/checkout

Each express option is its own button and its own tracked click, so if one payment method is quietly driving a disproportionate share of completed orders - or abandoned carts - that's visible instead of buried inside a single generic "checkout" event.

The Results

With a decade of continuous work behind it, Hawkeye Fan Shop can trust its numbers and count on the storefront when it matters most.

  • A storefront that holds up under sharp, gameday-driven traffic
  • Reporting that stakeholders can act on without a background in analytics
  • Product and catalog tracking detailed enough to guide real merchandising calls
  • A measurement setup carried forward, not rebuilt from scratch, as tools have changed

Business Impact

A retailer tied to a live sports calendar doesn't get a slow ramp-up, and a ten-year relationship doesn't run on guesswork either. Measurement has to keep pace with the catalog, the schedule, and the platform itself, all at once - and it has to hold together across channels, not just on the storefront itself.
